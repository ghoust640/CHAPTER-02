01

AFTERMATH

FORWARD BASE HALLOW · 06:40 · NINE HOURS AFTER

The report closes before breakfast. It’s nine lines long, and G7 wrote none of them.

That isn’t unusual. Operators produce a debrief; the debrief goes up; somewhere above them a man whose name isn’t on the distribution list turns it into a paragraph that can be read aloud. What’s unusual this morning is how little of the paragraph they recognise.

NIGHTGLASS · FILE CLOSURE · SUMMARY OF RECORD
OPERATION NIGHTGLASS — CONCLUDED. OBJECTIVE ACHIEVED.
WEAPONS RECOVERED: YES. TARGET CAPTURED: YES. UNIT LOSSES: NONE.
CIVILIAN CASUALTIES: NO MAJOR INCIDENT RECORDED.
ANOMALIES: NONE OUTSTANDING. FILE STATUS: CLOSED.
[G7 DEBRIEF ATTACHED AS APPENDIX — NOT SUMMARISED]

Patch reads it twice and then goes to sleep, because she has learned which fights are worth the hours. Sledge reads it once and says something that gets him moved along by a sergeant. Vanguard doesn’t read it at all.

The trouble isn’t that the report is generous. It’s that the report is tidy, and the night wasn’t. They were sent against six to eight men and met a fortified position: bricked windows, sandbagged bay, a machine gun on the gallery with a field of fire down the whole approach, and a chair beside it. Nobody brings a chair to an ambush. You bring a chair to a job you’ve been doing for weeks.

Four blocks of a district that never empties were emptied four hours before G7 was tasked. Politely. Without a shot.

Which leaves one sentence that nobody at Hallow wants to be the first to say out loud, so Whisper says it in the analysis bay instead, to a screen: somebody knew they were coming, and it wasn’t the people who were waiting.

02

THE NETWORK

SECTION 7 · ANALYSIS BAY · 11:20

The material recovered from Warehouse 6 fills two tables and a floor. Glitch takes the drives and Whisper takes the paper, and they work the way two people work when they’ve stopped pretending they’ll be finished by lunch.

By the third hour the shape has arrived, and it isn’t the shape anyone was briefed to expect.

An arms depot has weapons in it. This building had weapons in it, and also a filing system: shipping manifests going back six years, bank transfer records routed through four institutions and two currencies, blank identity stock in genuine municipal blanks, a contact register written in a commercial shorthand that Whisper says is used by freight brokers and nobody else. Ports on both sides of the Pacific. Three Vesperian harbours, one of which is military.

SCI is not a cell in the Sprawl with a grievance and a garage. SCI is a logistics company that also believes something, and the believing is the part the broadcasts talk about because the believing is the part that doesn’t implicate anybody upstairs.

Then Glitch finds the serials, and the room gets quiet in the specific way rooms get quiet when the work stops being interesting and starts being dangerous.

Ninety weapons out of Warehouse 6 carry Vesperian military serial blocks. Not filed off. Not overstamped. Not the mismatched civilian rubbish that turns up in every insurgency on earth, bought from someone’s cousin and re-blued in a shed. Service pattern, service serials, in sequence, in original wrap.

Weapons don’t walk out of a national armoury. They’re signed for. Every one of those ninety serials was signed for by somebody with an office, a stamp, and a pension.

03

THE INTERROGATION

HALLOW · DETENTION 2 · 14:05

The one they took alive at Warehouse 6 is a quartermaster, not a fighter, and he’s the calmest man in the building.

He has been given water and he drinks it. He answers to ALDER because that’s what the file says and he doesn’t seem to mind. He denies nothing at all, which frightens the interrogator more than anything he could have denied. Yes, SCI moves weapons. Yes, SCI moves Rust-Dust, and yes, he knows what it does to the districts it’s sold in, and no, he isn’t going to pretend that the money is clean.

Yes, civilians have died. He gives a number. It’s higher than the number in the state broadcast, and he gives it without being asked, which is how Rook knows it’s the true one.

[PAUSE — 4s]

[SUBJECT DECLINES FURTHER QUESTIONS. INTERVIEW SUSPENDED 14:22.]

The interrogator writes subject uncooperative, which is a strange thing to write about a man who has just confessed to trafficking.

Rook stands in the corridor afterwards and does the arithmetic that the report won’t. If the weapons aren’t SCI’s, and SCI was shipping them out of the country, then SCI wasn’t buying an arsenal. SCI was running a freight route for somebody else, and taking a cut, and the cut was paid in the guns they kept.

That’s not an insurgency. That’s a subcontract.

04

NOVA KAELEN

TERRACE 4 · 16:30 · NO UNIT PRESENT

Nobody in Terrace 4 calls it an operation. They call it Tuesday night, and they’re still cleaning up on Wednesday afternoon.

The shopkeeper on the corner of the market row has swept his glass into a pile and is measuring the empty frame with a piece of string, because the replacement pane will be cut by a man on Terrace 6 who works from measurements and does not come down. Two women scrub the transit platform where a body was, with a bucket and a brush and no expectation of help. The blood has gone into the rubber matting and won’t come out; by evening someone has laid a mat over it and the mat becomes permanent.

A municipal drone comes down the corridor at head height, and it isn’t taking faces today. Today it’s talking.

MUNICIPAL UNIT M-114 · PUBLIC ADDRESS 16:31:02
“THE VESPERIAN GOVERNMENT REMAINS COMMITTED TO MAINTAINING PEACE AND SECURITY IN ALL DISTRICTS.”
“HOSTILE ELEMENTS IN THIS SECTOR HAVE BEEN REMOVED. RESIDENTS MAY RESUME NORMAL ACTIVITY.”
“REPORT SUSPICIOUS PERSONS. YOUR COOPERATION PROTECTS YOUR NEIGHBOURS.”
[MESSAGE REPEATS ON A NINE-MINUTE CYCLE UNTIL 22:00]

Four children walk past the front of the destroyed storefront on their way from the school hours, single file, without looking at it. The one at the back is eating fried dough from the stall that never closes, and the man who fries it has not closed today either, because closing costs more than glass.

Ask anyone on this corridor about SCI and you will not get a slogan. You’ll get the shrug, and then, if you stand there long enough, you’ll get the arithmetic.

SCI took the district’s protection money for nine years and called it a levy. SCI sold Rust-Dust in the stairwell two blocks from the clinic that won’t treat the overdoses after ten. When SCI blew Substation 9 the lights went out here, not in the spires. Nobody on Terrace 4 is proud of them.

And yet: the clinic quota was set in a building none of them will ever enter. The woman taken for an administrative discrepancy hasn’t come back. The checkpoints doubled the morning after the raid, and they were not put on the roads leading down from the towers.

They don’t want SCI. They want the thing that made SCI possible to stop happening. Nobody has ever offered them that, and so they’re counted as sympathisers, which is administratively simpler and has the advantage of being unfalsifiable.

This time the intelligence is good. That’s worth saying, because it will not help.

The exchange is real, the manifest came out of their own seizure, the signals confirm it, and the timing is the timing of a shipment rather than the timing of a politician. Whisper is satisfied with it, which is rare enough that Sledge comments on it.

What nobody is satisfied with is the map. Yard 12 sits under Terrace 6 with four thousand people living in a two-hundred-metre radius, in structures that are structurally the same building as the yard. The rules of engagement come down two pages long, and the second page is about what G7 may not do.

“So we’re raiding a market with a lawyer on our shoulder,” Sledge says.

“We’re raiding a market where people live,” Patch says, and that’s the end of that conversation.

06

THE EXCHANGE

22:10 → 22:52 · YARD 12 · SUB-LEVEL

The yard is a loading floor under a housing terrace, lit by strings of work lamps that somebody pays for informally, and it’s busy the way a fish market is busy at four in the morning.

G7 comes in from the drainage side and holds for eleven minutes, and in those eleven minutes the whole shape of the mission changes, because they can see the floor and the floor is not what a target set is supposed to look like.

There are SCI men. Six of them, maybe eight, and Whisper can pick them out because they stand where you stand when you’re responsible for something. There are criminals: two crews who have nothing to do with any cause and are haggling about it openly. And there are perhaps thirty other people who are simply moving boxes for money, because a night’s carrying pays what a week at the refinery pays.

A boy of maybe fifteen wheels a hand truck past their position with a crate of encrypted radios on it. A woman with a clipboard and a cardigan is checking lots off a list. Somebody has brought a thermos. None of these people are combatants and all of them are committing a capital offence under Article 88.

Positive identification required. Rook holds the team for four more minutes trying to build a picture, and that’s the correct decision, and it costs them the initiative.

They move at 22:34 and it goes wrong in nine seconds, which is fast even by the standards of the year.

The first shot isn’t theirs and isn’t SCI’s. It comes from one of the criminal crews, from a man who has been robbed at an exchange before and has decided never again, and after that everybody on the floor is shooting at somebody and only about half of them know who.

Then somebody sets off a charge. It’s not tactical. It’s panic, or it’s a man trying to destroy evidence, and it goes off in the south bay under the ordnance pallets and lifts a hand truck into a lighting string, and the shipment starts to burn.

Rust-Dust burns green. Everybody who was on that floor will tell you about the colour.

Four thousand people live above a floor that’s now on fire, and the stairwell that serves them comes down into the north corner, and there are people in it, and the SCI men are going out through the drainage side with the documentation in a satchel.

Forty-two minutes of one floor, rebuilt from four helmet feeds and the seized lot sheet. The green bloom opening in the south bay from 22:36 is the shipment itself: burning Rust-Dust, plotted from thermal returns, which is why the contacts nearest it stop resolving. Positive identification peaks at thirty-five per cent before the first shot, collapses when the floor scatters, and reaches its highest value at 22:44, by which time the shipment is gone and the men who mattered are already through the drain. Scrub it and watch the number get good too late.

They get thirty-eight people out of the north stairwell and four more off the second-floor walkway, and Patch works on two of them on the concrete with the fire behind her throwing that green light on everything, which is the image Trace will not talk about afterwards.

The shipment burns to nothing, which is technically the objective.

The runners get out through the south drain with the satchel, which is technically not.

Nine SCI personnel are taken alive on the floor. Four of them turn out to be labourers with no affiliation whatsoever, and they’ll spend eleven weeks in detention establishing it.

07

THE CRATE

22:58 · SOUTH BAY · DURING EVACUATION

Rook finds it because he’s counting exits and it’s in the way.

It’s in the south bay with the ordnance pallets, half under a fallen lighting string, and it hasn’t burned because it’s built not to. Not a weapons crate. A logistics container: reinforced, gasketed, corner-bound, the pattern the army uses to move documents and sensitive stores between depots, and it’s old. The bindings are the old width. The stencil is the old font.

The lid is open and empty. Whatever came out of it went out through the drain in a satchel.

He photographs the identification block because his hands do it before he decides to, and he’s reading it while he does, and the reading is the part that stops him. He knows this format. He’s known it since he was fourteen years old, from a photocopy in a solicitor’s envelope, and he saw it again nine hours ago on a transit document that no longer exists in any register.

RECOVERED ITEM · RL-S12-CR1 · STENCIL TRANSCRIPTION TAP BARS TO ATTEMPT DECRYPT
CONTAINER: LOGISTICS, DOCUMENT AND SENSITIVE STORES, PATTERN 61
ISSUING SYSTEM: VDN CENTRAL SUPPLY — SCHEMA WITHDRAWN FROM SERVICE 1999
LAST RECORDED MOVEMENT: NO ENTRY — REGISTER RETURNS NULL, NOT DENIED
DEPOT PREFIX: MATCHES SERIAL BLOCK M-14 — SAME NON-EXISTENT DEPOT
CLASSIFICATION LINE: ARCLIGHT / G1 / 04

Same line. Not a similar line. Not the same family of line. The same five characters and the same two slashes, stencilled onto a crate that has been in continuous service for twenty-six years, in a yard under a housing terrace, being emptied by men who are paid by somebody in his own supply chain.

Rook has spent eighteen years being told that ARCLIGHT never happened. What he’s looking at is the thing ARCLIGHT was carried in, still working.

Whatever was buried in 1998 wasn’t buried. It was kept, and it has been running ever since, and somebody has been signing for it every ninety days.

An error would have gone the other way. If the ministry didn’t know the serials were Vesperian, the broadcast would have said weapons and left it there, because vagueness is free and specificity is work. Somebody read the serial annex, understood exactly what it meant, and added a word.

The lie isn’t the story. The lie is the adjective, and the adjective took effort.

Glitch checks the file at 09:00. Her serial annex is in the appendix, as Whisper predicted, and the appendix is marked technical, not for summary.

Two hundred and fourteen entries, and he reads all of them, because the machine will not answer a question you ask correctly and sometimes answers one you ask badly.

It takes four hours. It isn’t in the schema list. It’s in a footnote to an archived audit annexe from 2001, in a paragraph about disposal of superseded container stock, quoted because somebody in 2001 needed to explain why a line item didn’t balance.

“Material recovered from G1-04 was transferred under executive authority.”

One sentence, in the passive voice, in a footnote about a balance sheet.

Material recovered from G1-04. Recovered means somebody went and got it. Transferred means it went somewhere and is still there. Executive authority means no signature, no office, no register entry, and nobody to write to.

Rook reads it eleven times and then sits back, and the thing he feels isn’t grief and isn’t even anger. It’s the flat professional recognition of a supply chain. Recovered, transferred, retained, reissued. Twenty-six years of ninety-day courier codes. A crate in Yard 12 with the old stencil on it and nothing inside.

His father didn’t vanish into a tragedy. His father went into an inventory.

10 · FINAL

THE WALL

13:50 · HALLOW · MEMORIAL CORRIDOR

The corridor between the archive and the mess is the memorial, because Hallow was built by people who thought remembering should be on the way to somewhere.

It’s forty metres long and both walls are names. Etched, not printed, in blocks by unit and year, and there are so many that the eye gives up and starts reading them as a texture. Rook has walked past it twice a day for six years. Cleaners polish the lower third; the upper third is left, and the dust in the letters is what makes them legible.

He stops today, which he has never done, and he walks it slowly, and he finds the section he has never once looked for.

The blocks are in order. G4. G5. G6. His own, G7, with eleven names on it already and room left, and the room left is the part nobody mentions.

And between G6 and G7, in the correct place, the correct width, at the correct height, with the correct heading bar cut into the stone above it:

G1. And nothing.

Not a gap where the stone was left rough. Not an unfinished panel. The panel is dressed and polished and the heading is cut, and the field beneath it’s empty, and it has been kept clean.

Twenty-two years. Somebody has been dusting a blank wall for twenty-two years and nobody has ever asked why, because asking would mean noticing, and the whole architecture of this building is designed so that noticing is something you do alone.

They didn’t forget G1. Forgetting is passive; forgetting leaves marks; forgetting would have left a rough panel and an argument about funding. This was cut to size, mounted, and maintained.

His father’s unit wasn’t lost. It was removed, carefully, by people who were still on the payroll, and the space where it had been was left dressed and clean so that nothing would look wrong.

Rook stands in front of it until the corridor lights change to afternoon setting, and then he goes to find Whisper.
